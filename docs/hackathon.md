# DNHacks 2026 — Submission and Judging

Source: organizer information and judging criteria supplied by the team, including the later Slack clarification and James L's 9:57 PM website update. Later clarifications supersede earlier conflicting instructions.

## Submission

- **Deadline: Sunday, September 6, 2026, at noon. No exceptions.**
- Organizers will send the submission platform link by email and Slack on Saturday morning.
- The supplied information does not state the deadline's time zone.

Required submission materials:

- Project title
- Project description
- A few screenshots
- A short slideshow presentation submitted in the portal, including when using a website on stage
- Source code

A demo video link is optional and supplemental, according to the later Slack clarification.

Teams retain full rights to all code produced during the hackathon. Source code is available only to judges, as needed to verify technical depth. Other submission materials appear in the showcase, with visibility determined by the submission form's public or participant/judge setting.

## Judging and Demo Format

- Judging uses a science fair format, scheduled for Sunday, September 6, 12:30–3:30 PM. Organizers may end earlier to allow more deliberation.
- Each team should expect visits from three groups: its selected track's judges, Best Use of AI judges, and Best in Design judges.
- The AI prize assesses the project's AI capabilities, not the use of AI to build the project.
- After all three groups have judged the team, team members can visit other projects.
- Judging criteria will be available on the submission site. Tailor the demo to these criteria.
- **Demos must be no longer than 3 minutes.**
- Teams can use slides, a live demo, or other materials during their three-minute science-fair presentation.
- Track winners and runners-up must give a stage presentation during the closing ceremony to remain eligible for prizes.
- **Slides must be submitted.** The stage uses the organizer's computer; teams cannot connect their own computer to the screen.
- The 9:57 PM update also permits a **public website instead of slides on stage**, with one teammate controlling the website on the organizer's computer. Slides are still required in the portal.
- The supplied messages do not confirm embedded-video playback support on the stage computer.

## Prize Eligibility and Closing Ceremony

- **Submit by noon, present to judges during the science fair, and stay for winner announcements and stage presentations. Missing any of these requirements forfeits prize eligibility.**
- Only the eight track winners and runners-up qualify for the overall Grand Prize ($25,000) and Runner Up ($10,000).
- Best Use of AI and Best in Design each have a winner and runner-up, who also present on stage. A special award alone does not qualify a team for the overall prizes.
- Participants' Choice voting opens during stage presentations. Participants vote in the portal and cannot vote for their own project. This award alone does not qualify a team for the overall prizes.
- Prize payments occur after the event.

## Event Logistics

- Sunday breakfast starts at 8 AM. Lunch is at noon or earlier. Project demos start at 12:30 PM.
- Teams can re-enter the venue overnight with their badges.

## Main Prizes — 100 Points

Prizes and tracks: Grand Prize, Runner Up, Defense, Energy and Industrialization, Health and Public Service, and Open Category.

### Problem & Real-World Impact — 25 Points

Assess the problem and proposed solution:

- Is the problem important and clearly defined?
- Is it relevant to the selected category and the hackathon's mission?
- Could the idea have a meaningful positive impact within the selected track's theme?

### Technical Execution — 50 Points

Assess technical depth, originality, and final project polish:

- How technically strong is the implementation?
- Does it show meaningful engineering depth?
- Does the prototype work?
- Were difficult technical challenges solved?
- Is the approach novel or meaningfully better than existing solutions?
- Did the team solve the problem creatively instead of simply reproducing an existing product?

### Feasibility & Deployment Potential — 25 Points

Assess whether the project can move beyond the hackathon:

- Could it realistically be developed and deployed in the real world?
- Could it become a real company?
- Has the team considered cost, security, infrastructure, regulation, integration, and user adoption?

## Best Use of AI — 100 Points

### Novelty and Importance of AI to the Solution — 25 Points

- Is the use of AI novel?
- Is AI fundamental to the product, or could it easily be removed?

### Technical Sophistication — 50 Points

Assess the technical execution and depth of AI tooling:

- Did the team do something technically interesting beyond connecting an existing model to an interface?
- Did it show thoughtful model selection, orchestration, evaluation, fine-tuning, agent design, data work, inference optimization, or other substantive AI engineering?
- A simple wrapper around an existing model should generally score lower unless the team created meaningful innovation elsewhere in the AI system.

### Reliability, Evaluation & Trustworthiness — 25 Points

Assess the team's commitment to reliability and trustworthiness, and how well the solution achieves them:

- Does the AI reliably solve the proposed problem?
- Has the team considered relevant failure modes, hallucinations, security, and reliability?
- Has the team developed an appropriately extensive and well planned testing process?

## Best in Design — 100 Points

### Design Quality & Craft — 30 Points

- How beautiful and polished is the project?
- Has the team considered accessibility, including readability and color choice?
- Is the design novel and unique, or copied from well known designs?

### User Experience & Usability — 40 Points

Assess the experience, intuitiveness, and importance of the design to solving the problem: form follows function.

- Is the product intuitive?
- Does the design make a complicated task simpler?
- Does it help the technology accomplish its purpose?
- Is information presented clearly?
- Is the design critical to the project's success at solving the problem?

### Understanding of the User — 30 Points

Assess whether a specific, detailed understanding of the user guides the design and solution:

- Does the team have a clear target user persona?
- Is that persona detailed and well defined?
- Are there design decisions or components based on that persona?
- Does the design account for who will use the project in the real world?
